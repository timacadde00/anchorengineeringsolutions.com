5 signs your building needs a structural inspection

Most building owners only think about structural inspection after something has clearly gone wrong. But by the time a problem is visible, it has often been developing for months or years. Here are five warning signs Anchor's engineers tell clients never to ignore.

1. Cracks wider than a pencil line

Hairline cracks in plaster are usually cosmetic. But cracks wider than 3mm, especially diagonal cracks near windows and doors, can signal foundation movement or structural stress.

2. Doors and windows that stick

If doors and windows that used to open smoothly now stick or no longer sit square in their frames, the building may be settling unevenly.

3. Sagging or bouncy floors

A floor that visibly dips or feels springy underfoot may indicate weakened joists or beams that need immediate attention.

4. Water stains or damp patches

Persistent water stains on ceilings or walls can point to compromised waterproofing — which, left unchecked, corrodes reinforcement steel over time.

5. Visible corrosion on exposed steel

Rust stains bleeding through concrete, or visibly corroded steel on balconies and columns, are a clear signal that reinforcement is being compromised.

"None of these signs on their own mean a building is unsafe — but they all mean it is time to call an engineer, not a painter."
